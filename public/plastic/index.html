How much plastic is in your dairy?

Researchers went looking for microplastics in milk and cheese. They found them in every sample. Here is how the three compare, measured as microplastic particles per kilogram of product.

Source: npj Science of Food (2025). Pick a product to carry it through the charts below.

What kind of plastic is it?

Four polymers account for most of what the study identified. Hover, tap, or tab onto any one of them to see what it is and where it comes from.

Ripened cheese carries 1,857 MP/kg, the highest of the three.

Percentages are each polymer’s share of the 58 identifications these four account for. Silicone, polyacrylate and PVC also turned up, but far less often. The study reports this composition across its dairy samples as a whole rather than per product, so the mix shown here is the same whichever product you pick. The concentration is what changes.

Why does ripened cheese have more?

During cheese-making, whey is drained off. That concentrates everything left behind, microplastics included, into a smaller amount of cheese. It is not extra contamination. It is the same plastic in less food.

See more details: size, shape and colour

Size

Most particles are far too small to see or feel.

  • 51–100 µm 33.8%
  • 3–50 µm 19.9%
  • 101–150 µm 19.6%
  • Larger than 150 µm 26.7%

Shape

Shape points at where a particle came from.

  • Irregular fragments 77.4%
  • Fibres 22.2%
  • Beads 0.38%

Fragments come from plastic breaking down, as equipment and packaging wear. Fibres come from synthetic textiles, including the protective clothing worn during processing.

Colour

Colour is another clue about the source.

  • Grey 68.4%
  • Brown 15.0%
  • Black 9.8%
  • Other 6.8%

Grey particles point at industrial plastic components, the packaging and machinery the dairy passes through, rather than anything picked up from the wider environment.

What does this mean for me?

What we don’t know

The health effects of eating microplastics are not settled. Research is ongoing, and anyone telling you the answer with confidence is ahead of the evidence.

What we do know

Dairy is not a special case. You are almost certainly taking in microplastics through plenty of other foods too.

The choice

If you eat a lot of ripened cheese, you are getting more microplastics than someone who mostly drinks milk. That is worth knowing when you shop. It is not a reason to panic.
